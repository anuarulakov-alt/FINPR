An AI Answer for Kazakhstan

Why does foreign capital outside the resource value chain stay narrow and resource-adjacent in Kazakhstan, and would an AI investor-targeting tool be worth its costs?

1The Problem

Kazakhstan holds about $164bn of foreign direct investment (FDI), but outside oil, gas and mining the money that arrives is narrow and sits close to the resource base.

Scope

"Outside the resource value chain" can be read two ways. Narrow: investment in activities that do not process the country's own hydrocarbons or ores, so metallurgy, refining and petrochemicals do not count. Broad (the official reading): moving up the value chain into midstream and downstream processing counts as diversification. This site reports both, and the gap between the two readings is part of the finding.

Who is affected

The state's diversification agenda, which the OECD finds only partly fulfilled, and the firms and workers in non-resource sectors that would gain from foreign capital, technology and supplier links. This site does not claim to measure effects on particular regions or groups, because no data collected here would support it.

The social-science question

Why does foreign capital outside the resource value chain stay small and resource-adjacent in Kazakhstan? Is the binding constraint information and matching frictions (H1), crowding-out by the resource sector (H2), or weak credible commitment and state discretion (H3)? The AI question comes second: under what conditions would an AI investor-targeting tool address it?
  • H0 Market size, distance and factor costs explain the pattern. No data was collected on this, so the site cannot rule it out.
  • H1 Information and matching frictions. The only hypothesis an AI tool could touch, and no public evidence for it was found.
  • H2 Crowding-out by the resource sector (Dutch disease, competition for rents).
  • H3 Weak credible commitment and state discretion.

3Current State of Research

Research agrees that resource wealth shapes who invests and on what terms, but disagrees on whether promotion, linkages or institutions are the lever.

ApproachWhat it claimsSourcesHow far I read
The gapIn a non-systematic search, I found (i) no Kazakhstan-specific evidence on whether investors outside resources stall at partner, site or supplier matching; (ii) no causal evaluation of AI-based investor targeting; and (iii) investment-promotion estimates only from small open economies, chiefly Costa Rica, which cannot separate information frictions from rent and commitment constraints in a state-heavy resource economy.

Limit: the search was not systematic (no Scopus or Google Scholar pass, no Russian-language literature), so the gap is a working statement, not a finding.

4The AI-Assisted Solution

An AI tool that ranks foreign firms and sectors by their likelihood to invest outside the resource base, to help the investment promotion agency prioritise outreach.

What it does
Ranks candidate firms and sectors for outreach. A human officer reads the ranking and decides.
Inputs
Public firm and sector data, public project announcements, relatedness data. Non-public agency records are not assumed; if they were used, consent and disclosure would become a cost (see the Bill).
Output
A ranked list with the reasons for each rank.
Who decides
The agency's staff, not the model.
How it is evaluated
Against a holdout group and a non-AI baseline, with an objective that rewards sector novelty rather than resemblance to past investors.
What it does not touch
Energy prices, courts, tax rules and market size (H0, H2, H3).

Documented cases

Only one case reports an outcome against a comparison, and its source co-ran the pilot. The evidence base is thin, and that is itself a finding.

5The Bill: what it would cost

The licence fee is the smallest cost. The real bill is mistargeting, state discretion and what the money could have fixed instead. Red lines mark where no data exists.

6Verdict

Only under conditions. Not a plain yes, and not a plain no.

Knowing the costs, Kazakhstan should not adopt an AI investor-targeting tool on current evidence. It should run it as a small, evaluated pilot, and only if the conditions below are met first. If the real obstacle is energy, courts or market size, the tool treats a symptom and the money is better spent elsewhere.

Conditions

ConditionStatusNote

The strongest case for "yes"

A ranking tool is cheap and a human still decides. Costa Rica's pilot reports a gain over the agency's own earlier method, and investment-promotion research finds that agencies can raise first entry for firms facing information frictions. Even a modest gain is worth the small cost, and a pilot with a holdout group would produce the missing evidence.

The strongest case for "no"

The evidence points away from information as the binding constraint, and the pilot evidence is not independent and has no outcome baseline. A model trained on decades of oil and gas investors favours investors like the past, which works against diversification. A tool that looks objective can also hide state discretion about who counts as a priority investor.

What I accepted

The problem framing around composition and narrowness, the three hypotheses (H1 to H3) plus a baseline H0, the narrow and broad definitions, the use of gross flows and stock as headline numbers, the verification labels on every number, and the verdict "only under conditions".

What I rejected, and why

I rejected the first candidate ranking, because it rewarded problems that suit AI. I dropped the claim that Invest KOREA "doubled reinvestment rates", because the source reports a hit rate and not a causal effect. I did not count Invest Estonia as a documented case, because it reports no outcomes. I stopped treating negative net flow excluding reinvested earnings as evidence of a non-resource shortfall, because the sector split shows that sign comes from mining.

Method limits

Claude's fetch tool returned summaries of pages, not the original files, so every number here is hollow until checked in the primary PDF. Literature was not searched systematically. Several sources were read as abstracts only. Some shares are my arithmetic. The environmental, labour and governance costs have large gaps, shown as "no data". All of this is shown on the site, not hidden.
